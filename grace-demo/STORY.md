## 1. The premise in one breath

Twelve years ago it rained on the small mountain kingdom of **Lodmir** for forty days, and under the rain the
**Margrave of Hal** laid siege to the **Swan Fortress**. Old **King Bolesław** died on the nineteenth day. On the
twentieth night his son **Radomir** rode out alone to the **Woman in the Wood** and made a bargain he has never told
anyone: *"When the rain comes back, I will come for what you hold dearest. Until then the kingdom is yours."* By
morning the Margrave's army was gone. Nobody knows where it went.

The game begins on the evening the rain comes back.

The engine of the story is that sentence. The king doesn't know what "what you hold dearest" means: his crown, his
kingdom, his wife, one of his children, himself. **The player decides it, without being told,** by what they spend
the king's weeks on.

The second engine arrives with a letter: **King Gniewomir of Krasna**, the great lowland kingdom to the south, will come
to the **Swan Festival** to sign a pact. Lodmir needs that pact (Hal is arming again), and Lodmir cannot hold a festival
in the rain. So Radomir goes back to the Wood a second time, and buys seven dry days. *"Seven days of sun,"* says the
Woman, *"for a king's breath."* He thinks she means his own.

On the last night of the festival, at the feast in the Swan Fortress, King Gniewomir drinks from the loving cup and dies.
The rain starts again before his body is cold. His son is camped in the meadow with three hundred knights, and gives
Radomir until his father is buried in Krasna to name the killer. The kingdom that came to sign a pact will come back to
burn the Swan Fortress, unless the king finds out who did it, and what it will cost him to say so.

**Tone:** warm, wry, tired, sometimes funny, folk-tale dark at the edges. Court is a comedy of small disasters; the
family is the heart; the festival is loud and bright; the Wood is quiet and wrong; the murder is a slow tightening.

## 4. The shape of the game

**Weeks.** Each week: free scenes (walk the castle, talk to the family, read letters in the study, visit the chapel, the
yard, the wood), **court** in the throne room (petitions, agents and items), then **the week's ledger**. The festival week
replaces court with the festival (§6); the crisis weeks add the investigation (§7).

**The rain is the clock.** Forty days of rain, counted only while it rains. The festival's seven dry days don't count: the
Woman is holding them. The count is visible as how hard it rains on the windows, and in what Kazik's crow says.

**Ledgers.**
- *Kingdom:* Gold, Stores, Mood (built), Army (new).
- *Family:* Halina's trust, Zofia's respect, Kazik's closeness (0–10 each, never shown as numbers; shown as how they
  speak to you and where they stand when you walk in).
- *Krasna's patience* (chapters 4–5): the days left, and how Mściwoj greets you. Wrong accusations and stalling cost days.
- *Rumour* (from chapter 2): how much the town says about the king's nights. Fed by careless visits to the House of the
  Lantern (§9) and by the saint's confessional; read by Halina, by Canon Leopold, and by Mściwoj when he wants a weapon.

**What the Woman takes** at the end is the ledger the player valued most (§11), and the festival bargain decides *which*
king she was talking about.

## 5. Chapter beat boards

Each card: **where · who · what happens · the choice · what it moves**.

### Chapter 1 — *The Rain Returns* (weeks 1–2)

1. **Bedchamber · King** — evening, first rain on the window. The crown on its cushion. (Built.)
2. **Throne room · court** — Marta and the mill road, Tomasz and the tolls. (Built.) The Widows of the Siege (§8).
3. **Garden · Halina, Zofia, Kazik** — the family introduced. *Kazik: "The big crow says she's coming when it rains."*
4. **Training yard · Bogdan, Zofia** — Zofia caught sparring. **Choice:** forbid · allow under Bogdan · train her yourself.
5. **Chapel · Dorota** — the saint, the queen's cold eye, the priest. (Built.) Flirting here opens the confessional and
   starts the Rumour ledger.
6. **Study · letter** — a letter in the old king's hand behind the portrait: *"If the rain comes back, do not go to her
   again."* The king burns it or keeps it (**choice**; keeping it lets Halina find it in chapter 5). A second sheet is
   folded inside it, in the same hand: *"Wanda keeps my other key."* (§9)
7. **Throne room · the Krasnian herald** — King Gniewomir will come to the Swan Festival at midsummer "to renew the
   friendship of our fathers". **Choice:** host the festival (Gold −, Mood ++, the Pact possible) · plead the rain (the
   Pact is lost; Krasna cools; chapter 3 becomes a smaller embassy instead of the festival).

### Chapter 2 — *Grain and Debt* (weeks 3–4)

1. **Bedchamber · Halina** (night) — she wants the League's grain; she wants to know why he flinches at the rain. **Choice:**
   tell her part of the truth · lie · say nothing. (The night scene follows if she's willing. Built.)
2. **Garden · Kazik** — sicker, cheerful; he's teaching a crow words. *"It says your name wrong."*
3. **Recruit Dobrawa** — the herbalist treats Kazik in the garden and recognises a black feather knotted in red thread.
4. **Court** — festival petitions (§8): Bartosz and the fire licence, the Marshal of the Lists and the masked knight,
   Canon Leopold and the saint.
5. **The fostering choice** — send Kazik to Velka (Halina ↑, Kazik ↓, the League friendly) · keep him (the reverse).
6. **Ilka's report (study)** — Konrad of Hal is buying swords in Velka's markets with League credit.
7. **The Wood · the second bargain** — the festival needs sun. The Woman sells seven dry days for *"a king's breath."*
   **Choice:** take it · refuse (the festival goes ahead in the rain: half the activities, Mood barely moves, and the joust
   is fought in mud). Pet the cat either way.
8. *(Optional, from the letter)* **The chapel crypt · the siege tunnel** — behind the old king's tomb, a door and a key
   that fits it. It comes out under the House of the Lantern in the river quarter. Wanda has been waiting twelve years
   for someone to use it again. The house opens (§9).

### Chapter 3 — *The Swan Festival* (week 5: three days and a night)

The festival week is its own mode: no court; three days on the festival grounds and one night in the great hall. Full
plan in §6.

1. **Day 1 · the Fairground** — the guests arrive in procession; Gniewomir embraces Radomir like a son and calls him
   "Bolesław's boy"; the fair; the fire-spitters; the bear. Evening: the first Pact talk in Gniewomir's pavilion.
2. **Day 2 · the Lists** — Dorota's Blessing of the Lists; the melee (Bogdan against Ser Ottokar); the archery (Zofia in
   the junior bouts); the Black Swan Knight unhorses three knights. Evening: the Pact's terms (§6.4). **Choice.**
3. **Day 3 · the Joust and the Pavilions** — Mściwoj against the Black Swan Knight; the crowning of the Queen of Love and
   Beauty (**choice:** Halina · Queen Wiesława · Saintess Dorota · Marta the miller's widow, which the crowd loves); the
   pavilions at dusk.
4. **The night · the Feast** — the great hall dressed for a feast. The Black Swan Knight unmasks: Konrad of Hal, at
   Radomir's own table, under guest-right. The Salamanders spit fire between the courses. The two kings share the Swan
   Cup: the host drinks first from the left lip, the guest from the right. Gniewomir laughs, drinks, puts the cup down,
   and dies with his hand still on it. Outside, it begins to rain.

### Chapter 4 — *The Dead King* (weeks 6–7)

1. **The hall, that night** — Mściwoj, King of Krasna for an hour, with his father in his arms. His knights at the doors.
   **Choice:** keep the doors closed (no one leaves; Krasna's patience −, the killer is still inside) · open them (the
   guests scatter; clues go with them).
2. **The ultimatum** — twelve days, the time it takes to carry a king home to Krasna and bury him. Then three hundred knights
   become twenty thousand. Court continues, but every petition is about the crisis (§8).
3. **The investigation** — clues in eight places (§7). Agents can be sent; the king can go himself. The House of the
   Lantern becomes essential: the Krasnian retinue spent the festival nights there.
4. **The Woman** — the hut. *"I sold you sunshine. A king stopped breathing. You think one paid for the other."*
5. **The accusation** — the throne room, before Mściwoj. **Choice:** name a killer (§7.4), or take the blame and pay.

### Chapter 5 — *The War of the Cup* (weeks 8–9)

What the accusation made: war with Krasna, war with Hal, or a peace with a price (§7.5). Mustering in the yard; the Pact
signed or torn up; Halina and Jarek; Konrad's search for his father's army comes to the Wood. If the letter was kept,
Halina finds it now: **Bedchamber · Halina** — the worst conversation of their marriage.

### Chapter 6 — *Forty Days* (week 10)

1. **The walls** — whoever is at war with Lodmir is at the walls in the rain: the title screen made real. Siege days
   replace court: each day a "petition" from the walls (a breach, fire, sickness, desertion).
2. **The fortieth night** — the rain stops at once. The Woman walks through the camp and nobody stops her. She comes up the
   hill and into the place the player's ledgers point to (§11).

Three grounds side by side, joined by gates you walk through (no loading screen between them, only the fade), plus the
great hall for the feast. The map gets a **Swan Meadow** pin during the festival week.

### 6.2 What the festival moves

Mood rises every day the fair is open (more with sun). The Pact's terms move with the talks. Every family member has a
moment: Halina gets one quiet walk through the fair with the king; Zofia shoots; Kazik and the bear. Dorota's blessing is
the most public thing she does all game, and the queen watches the king watch her.

### 6.3 What the player can set up without knowing

The feast is the murder's stage, and the player sets the stage: the seating plan, whether Konrad is let in under
guest-right, whether the fire-spitters perform indoors (the moment every eye leaves the high table), whether the king
spends the interval with Dorota in the chapel (then her alibi is the king himself, and he can't give it without saying
why). None of these change who the killer is; they change which clues exist.

### 6.4 The Pact of the Pass

Gniewomir offers Krasna's shield against Hal for the Swan Pass: Krasnian garrisons in the two pass forts, his road toll-free
through Lodmir, Lodmir's levies at his call. Talks can trim it (no levies; one fort; a term of years). The king's choice on
day 2: **sign tonight · sign after the feast · refuse.** Ilka argues hardest against it.

## 7. The Dead King: the murder

### 7.1 The truth

**Ilka** killed King Gniewomir. She served old Bolesław through the siege and has served the Swan ever since, and she
believes the Pact is the end of Lodmir: Krasnian garrisons in the pass are a knife at the kingdom's throat for a hundred
years, and Radomir was going to sign. She knew the loving-cup custom (every Lodmiri does; no Krasnian does), so she put
**wolf's hood** (dried aconite) on the guest's lip of the Swan Cup while the hall watched the fire-spitters. To turn
Krasna's grief on Hal, she slipped an old Hal officer's signet, kept twelve years in her archive since the siege, into
the cupbearer's sleeve. Her plan: Krasna and Lodmir together against Hal, with no pact.

### 7.2 The clues, and where they are

### 7.3 The red herrings (each guilty of something else)

- **Konrad of Hal** hated Gniewomir, who refused him, and he was at the feast under a mask. Innocent of the cup. He came to
  learn where his father's army went, and he will follow that question into the Wood in chapter 5.
- **Jarek of Velka** loses his river if the Pact is signed, and League warehouses buy rat poison by the sack. He is guilty
  of skimming the Kalina tolls (Tomasz's petition, §8), not of murder. Accusing him breaks Halina's heart.
- **Queen Wiesława and Ser Ottokar** left the hall together during the fire. No alibi, and they lie about it. They are
  guilty of adultery (Lena at the House of the Lantern knows), not of murder.
- **Saintess Dorota** blessed the cup before the toast; Agata at the House of the Lantern, who left Dorota's convent, will
  say she's capable of anything. Innocent; and if the king was with her in the interval, her alibi is the king.
- **The Woman** sold sun for "a king's breath." She didn't touch him. *"I didn't need to. Kings are always being killed by
  someone. I only knew which week."*

### 7.4 The accusation

In the throne room, before Mściwoj, with the court watching. The king needs enough clues to make a name stick; with too
few, Mściwoj laughs and the days run out.

| Name | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Ilka** (true; needs clues 1, 3 and 6 or 8) | Ilka confesses, calmly: she did it for Lodmir. **Choice:** hand her to Krasna (she's executed; peace; the kingdom loses its eyes and the one person who knew about the first bargain) · let her take the poison she carries (peace; Mściwoj is cheated of his justice and doesn't forget) · let her escape through the tunnel (Krasna's patience collapses unless the king pays blood-price) |
| **Konrad** (false, but Ilka's plan) | Mściwoj believes it: Krasna and Lodmir against Hal. But Krasna's help has a price, and it is the Pact, signed in the rain |
| **Jarek** (false) | the League embargoes the river; Halina's trust falls through the floor; Krasna is satisfied only for a season |
| **Wiesława and Ottokar** (false) | Mściwoj's own family: he kills Ottokar in the hall, and turns on Lodmir for the insult when the truth comes out |
| **The Woman** (false) | Mściwoj sends men into the Wood. Few come back. The Woman's price at the end goes up |
| **Take the blame** | blood-price: the pass and a chest of gold; peace, and a poorer, smaller Lodmir |
| **No one** | war with Krasna when the twelve days run out |

## 9. The House of the Lantern

### 9.1 Why a king goes there

Kings don't visit brothels; the town would know by morning. Radomir goes because of his father:

- Old King Bolesław dug a tunnel in the siege, from the chapel crypt to the cellars of a house in the river quarter, to
  send messengers out when the gates were shut. After the siege he kept using it, for himself. The house belonged then, and
  belongs now, to **Wanda**, who was his mistress for eleven years and who still keeps his other key (the letter, §5 ch. 1).
- The king finds the tunnel in chapter 2 (optional). Wanda treats him first as his father's son and then as a customer
  with a crown he isn't wearing. In the house he is **the Falconer**: hooded, in a plain cloak, the crown left in the crypt.
- In chapter 4 the house becomes the investigation's centre: Ilka herself tells the king that the Krasnian retinue drank
  there every night of the festival, and that Wanda's women hear what men won't tell a priest. (She doesn't know that
  Wanda's ledger has her name in it.)

### 9.2 The rules of the house

- Everyone in it is an adult and chooses to be there. The women are workers with their own reasons, prices, opinions and
  limits; each can say no, and some will, until they trust him.
- Every intimate scene is behind the 18+ gate (`NIGHT.confirm`), and the player picks the king's lines. The king's penis
  is never shown.
- **Rumour.** Each visit is a risk. Coming and going by the tunnel is safe; being seen in the common room by a Krasnian
  squire, a League clerk or Canon Leopold's nephew is not. When Rumour fills, the story breaks in court: Halina hears it in
  public, and in chapter 4 Mściwoj uses it.
- **Trust.** Each woman has her own. Talk, buy her time without asking for anything, help with her trouble (each has one),
  or be with her. Clues and personal endings unlock with trust, not with sex; sex speeds some of them and slows others.

### 9.5 Scenes

Each woman's scene uses the night scene's machinery (§13c of `ENGINE.md`): the gate, the choice strip, undressing, the pose
driver, the stroke, the sounds. Positions so far are the bed's (kissing, riding, missionary, from behind) and the
confessional's standing pose; the house adds standing against a wall (Nadia), the bath (Lena), and Wanda's chair. Each
scene ends with the woman's own line about the king, which changes with her trust, and some trust gains open her clue.

## 11. Endings

The Woman's collection (what the king holds dearest) is crossed with the crisis's outcome (who is at the walls).

| Ending | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|

| **B · The Empty Cradle** | the family's highest trust is Kazik's, and the kingdom is strong | the Woman takes Kazik, gently; he goes with her as if he knew (the bear walks beside them). The rain ends. The crows stay at the fountain |
| **C · The Drowned Crown** | the kingdom neglected (Stores and Army low) | she takes the kingdom: the rain never stops, the river takes the lower town, the family leaves over the mountains together. The swan banner left on the empty throne |
| **D · The Bargain Broken** | Dobrawa, Anzelm and Babka Hela heard, the true name found, Halina told the truth before chapter 5, **and Ilka named** | the king calls the Woman by her name; she has to bargain again. She takes the rain itself and twelve years of the king's life (he ages on screen). Everyone lives |
| **E · Two Kings' Breath** | the festival bargain taken, and the wrong name given in chapter 4 | the Woman explains her price at last: she was owed *a* king's breath, and the dead king's was taken by someone else, so she takes Radomir's at the fortieth night. Every ending ends in the garden, the place the family meets. The crisis colours each one: under a Krasnian banner, a Hal
banner, or the swan.

## 12. Systems the story needs

- **Calendar:** *built (CAL, game/g45)*: the phases (the court day, the festival's three days, the twelve days of the crisis), "end the
  day" from the bed (sleep, or the night with Halina), the bouts on their days, the meadow pin by phase, rain in the crisis.
  Still to come: weeks and court days between, the rain as a counted clock.
- **Flags and meters:** story flags, family trust, Army, Krasna's patience, Rumour, each woman's trust at the house.
- **Dialogue choices in conversation:** `NIGHT.ask` exists for scenes; court and talk need the same strip.
- **Event scheduler:** scenes that trigger on entering a room in a given week with given flags.
- **Letters and a journal:** readable documents in the study; the investigation's clue board.
- **The festival mode:** three days with a day's activities each, the grounds connected by gates, the feast.
- **Outfits:** the Falconer's cloak (the king without crown or tabard), the feast's finery, Dorota's white.
- **Shows:** the fire-spitters, the duel choreography, the joust's painted horses, the puppet play.
- **Siege mode:** court variant with daily wall events.
- **Endings:** five end sequences using the cutscene system.
